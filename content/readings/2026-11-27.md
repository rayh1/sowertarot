## The card

A travelling player stands at the end of his stage-cart after the show. He holds a carved mask out in his right hand and looks at it. The face he looks with is his own: bare, calm and open. Nothing is painted on it, and nothing about it is performing.

Around him the work of the day lies scattered. Three more masks rest on the boards, one serene, one smiling, one sorrowful. The red curtain-cloth hangs off its rail. Beyond the cart, the village green and its cottages wait under soft clouds. The audience has gone home.

## Essence

The Mask is the card of the persona: the faces you wear to meet the world, and the self who wears them. Each role you play is honest and shows something true about you, but none of them is all of you. The card does not ask you to throw the masks away or to hide behind them. It asks you to hold each one far enough from your face that you can see it, and to remember whose hand is holding it.

## For today

Look at the player's face, not the mask. It is the quietest thing on the card, and the most familiar. He did not have to search for it. It was there under every role all evening, waiting for the curtain to come down.

So today's question is: what does your own face feel like when it is playing no one? You have known that face longer than any role. Most days it gets buried under the next call, the next errand, the next person who needs something from you.

Once today, in a gap between two roles, notice your face from the inside. The set of your jaw, the line of your brow, what your mouth is doing. Let it come back to how it rests when nobody is watching. Then go into the next role from there, so that it is that face, and not the last mask, that picks up the next one.
