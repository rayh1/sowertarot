## The card

A woman in a sky-blue cloak stands on a grassy ridge at sunset, half turned from us, her face in profile. Below her a golden sea runs all the way to a flat line where a round orange sun is resting. Four dark birds cross the sky on the right, and one bright star has come out in the upper left.

Look down, though, before you look out. Pale boulders lie half sunk in the amber grass around her feet, and a rocky outcrop rises beside her. They are old, heavy and still. She can stand on that ridge and take in the whole wide view only because the stone under the grass is holding her there.

## Essence

The Horizon is the edge between what you know and what you don't, the line that moves back as you move toward it. It stands for aspiration: the pull forward that shapes a life, not because you will arrive but because you keep facing a direction. Upright, the card says that pull is healthy. You do not need to reach the edge to grow; the edge grows as you do.

## For today

Today the question is not where you are heading but what you are standing on. The view only opens because you have climbed to a ridge, and the ridge is made of things you already have: skills you learned the slow way, people who have stayed, habits that still hold when you are tired. These are the boulders in the grass. They are easy to overlook because they never move.

Pick one of them and name it plainly. Then ask what it lets you see that you could not see without it. Whatever you want next will be reached from that footing, not from somewhere new.

If the far line feels too far today, stop looking at it for a minute. Put your weight on what is solid, and let the view stay where it is. It will still be there when you lift your eyes again.
