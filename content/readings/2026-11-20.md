## The card

At the edge of a night sea, a barefoot woman in an indigo dress pours water from a silver bowl back into the incoming waves. A full moon shines in a clear sky above a bank of purple clouds, and rocks line the shore.

Look down at the sand by her feet. Starfish lie there, left by the last tide. The water carried them in and went back out without them. They are what the sea leaves behind when it pulls away: proof that the ebb gives something too.

## Essence

The Tide is the rhythm of coming and going. Water arrives and recedes, is received and given back, and both movements belong to the same life. Its wisdom is not to stop the water but to move with it, knowing that what goes out returns and what comes in will leave again. Reversed, the rhythm sticks. The sea has gone out, and you stay fixed on the empty water, so the bared sand looks only like loss.

## For today

Think of something that has drawn back lately: a busy season ending, a role you no longer hold, a friendship gone quieter, energy lower than it was. Reversed, the card shows you staring at the empty water, counting what went out. The ebb feels like falling behind, as if the life you were reaching for has moved further away.

It has not moved further away. What you reach for travels with you, and it kept pace through the low water too. What changed is what you hold now. The tide left things on the sand that were not there before it came in.

So go and find them before the next wave covers them. Name three things that last season left you: a skill you had to learn, a person you met through it, a truth about what you can and cannot carry. Then pick one and put it to work on something you still want. Use the skill on a new problem, or write to that person with a new idea. Let the starfish be the first thing you carry forward, not a souvenir of the high water.
