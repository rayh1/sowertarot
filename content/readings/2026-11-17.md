## The card

A figure sits in a dim room, face hidden in shadow, one open hand held near a clay bowl. In the bowl a single ember glows orange and crimson in a bed of white ash. Its light is small. It warms the hand but never reaches the walls.

On the left, a doorway leads out into darkness. Nothing beyond it shows. On the right, a tall window holds a pale sky over hills and pine forest, the outside world kept at a distance. The figure faces neither. All of the attention goes to the bowl.

## Essence

The Ember stands for potential at rest, neither dead nor fully alight. It asks for care: shelter the glow, know when to feed it, know when it is spent. The ember stage has its own power, but it is a stage. Protection is half of the work. Commitment is the other half.

## For today

Reversed, the tending has become a reason to stay put. The room is warm near the bowl and dark everywhere else, so you sit closer. The idea, the skill or the hope you keep alive has turned into the thing that keeps you home. Every outing looks like a risk to the coal, and the doorway stays an empty shape at the edge of the room.

Yet an ember can be carried. People used to bring a live coal from one house to the next in a clay pot to light a new hearth. The glow does not need to stay where it started in order to survive. Often it needs the change of air.

Where would the thing you guard go if it left the room? Pick a place it has never been, and step through with it today. Bring the notebook to the meeting. Play the song for someone outside the family. Sign up for the course that meets in a building you have never entered. What lies past the door stays dark until you are on the other side of it. That is the reason to go.
