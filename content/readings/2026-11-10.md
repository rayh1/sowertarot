## The card

A crown sits on a plain wooden table. We look down into it, and the first thing the eye finds is its inside: a ring of fine gold around nothing at all. Beside it lies a heap of red cloth, tied with a blue band, dropped rather than folded.

Behind the table, a heavy door stands open. Someone in an ochre travelling coat has just stepped through it into a bright field. They walk without hurry toward a single bare tree, and the pale morning light fills the doorway behind them.

## Essence

The Hollow Crown is power handed back. The crown is whole and well made; nobody broke it or took it. Its owner saw that the role had grown heavier than what it was meant to guard, and chose to set it down. This is an ending made in clarity, not defeat, and the freedom it opens lies on the far side of a door that once seemed shut.

## For today

Look down into the crown, as the card invites. Most roles we carry, at work or at home, look solid from the side: the title, the duty, the part we always play. From above, some of them turn out to be a shape around a gap. What role in your life would look like that, if you looked straight into it?

The answer is probably not new. Somewhere you noticed it already and kept the thought quiet. Today, give that thought room. Sit still for a while, without a list of pros and cons, and let it say itself plainly. Recognising it is enough for now; the table and the door can wait.

If it helps, name the role in one sentence and write down what it was once meant to protect. Then see whether that thing still needs the crown, or whether it would do fine in a plain coat.
