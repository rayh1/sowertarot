## The card

A clay bowl sits in a dark room, filled with white ash. In the middle of the ash rests one ember, its surface veined black and red like cooling lava. A figure with a shadowed face holds an open hand toward it, close enough to feel the heat, while the other hand rests on a knee.

The ash is easy to miss. It is the pale part, the part that already burned. Yet it is what the ember lies in. It keeps the air off and the heat in, and without it the small coal would cool on bare clay. Smoke rises in one even line, neither growing nor fading.

## Essence

The Ember stands for potential that waits between going out and catching fire. It asks for shelter first and commitment later, and for the judgment to know what can be lit again and what must be released. The ember stage is not weakness. It is a held state, and it holds because of what surrounds it.

## For today

Reversed, the care narrows. You watch only the red point and treat everything pale as waste. Old drafts, past attempts, work that ended: you want them swept off so the live part can stand clean. But the glow has nothing to rest in without them. Cleared too thoroughly, a coal sits exposed and fades.

What finished piece of your past is still holding up the thing you care about now? An earlier version that taught you the shape, an attempt that failed but showed where the heat was, a person whose old help the current plan still stands on.

Find that piece and put it back where it belongs. Open the old file and set it next to the new one. Write what the failed attempt proved, in one line, at the top of the current plan. Thank the person by name. Nothing new gets added. The parts you already have simply start to hold each other.
