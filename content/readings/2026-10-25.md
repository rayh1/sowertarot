## The card

A man sits on a low bench at a field table on a hilltop, his back half turned to us. At his feet a leather satchel lies in the grass. It is packed. He has walked this land, and he will walk it again, but for now the bag is down.

Because he has stopped, the work can happen. On the parchment a river takes shape that matches the river in the valley below. A brass compass holds one corner of the sheet. The late sun is low and warm, and nothing around him is in a hurry.

## Essence

The Cartographer turns the wide unknown into something you can find your way through. He goes out into uncertain country, then comes back and writes down what he found, honestly marking what is known and what is still blank. The map is for others as much as for himself, and it will outlast the trip that made it.

## For today

Look at the satchel. Walking gathers what you know, but walking alone does not sort it. The sorting happens when you stop, sit somewhere set apart, and let the miles settle into lines on a page.

So ask yourself: what have you been carrying around all week without ever putting it down to look at? A problem you keep circling, notes in your head, a lesson from a hard week.

Today, choose a quiet spot and a fixed stretch of time where nothing new comes in. No messages, no next step. Set the bag down, take out one thing you have gathered, and draw it plainly: what you know, and where the map is still empty. Then rest a little before you pick the bag back up.
