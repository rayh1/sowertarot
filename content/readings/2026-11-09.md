## The card

A travelling player stands at the end of a small wooden stage-cart, his own face bare and calm. He holds one carved mask out at arm's length and studies it. Further along the boards lie three more: one pale and serene, one ochre and smiling, one pale and sorrowful. They rest side by side, close enough to touch, next to a red curtain-cloth taken down from its rail.

Behind the cart lies a village green with thatched cottages and a line of trees. The show is over. The masks are no longer on anyone's face, so for once they can be seen together, as a set.

## Essence

The Mask is the card of the persona: the faces a person wears to meet the world, and the self who wears them. A single life holds many honest roles, such as parent, worker, friend and stranger. Each one shows something true. The card asks neither to tear the masks off nor to hide behind them, but to hold each one far enough away to choose it freely, and to stay clear about whose hand is holding it.

## For today

Look at the row of masks on the boards. The calm one, the smiling one and the sorrowful one were all worn by the same player, in the same village, often on the same day. They were never separate shows. The patience you bring home from work, or the sharpness you carry out of a hard conversation, travels with you into the next role.

So today's question is this: where does one of your roles spill into another, and is that what you want? Perhaps the colleague who gets your best attention leaves little for a friend. Perhaps the tenderness you show a child is exactly what a tense meeting needs.

Pay attention to one role at a time, and then to the space between them. When you set one mask down and pick up the next, pause for a breath and decide what you carry across. Small care at each change of face shapes the whole of your day.
