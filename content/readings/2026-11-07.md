## The card

A pilgrim in an orange travelling coat leans on a walking stick and walks away from us down a long dirt road. The road winds back and forth across green foothills toward a small golden chapel at the foot of purple mountains. The light is dawn, calm and clear.

Along the roadside stand small cairns of stacked stone. Someone set each stone there by hand, to mark the way for whoever came next. The pilgrim passes them with his eyes on the far chapel.

## Essence

The Pilgrim is the seeker who chooses the long road on purpose. He leaves comfort behind and lets the walking itself change him. Reversed, that resolve turns inward. The journey becomes a private test of endurance, walked as though no one had gone this way before. The cairns are still there, but he has stopped reading them. Discipline hardens into stubbornness, and the call that once drew him on becomes a reason to keep apart.

## For today

Where are you treating your road as yours alone, when others have already marked it?

Look for the stones others left. A colleague who solved this problem last year, a friend who went through the same change, a note in an old file. Their marks are small and easy to walk past. Stop at one and read it. Let it adjust your step.

Then set a stone of your own. Tell someone near you what you learned on the stretch you just walked. Your pace takes its cue from the people closest to you, and theirs from you. The chapel stays far off; the road is lighter when it is shared.
