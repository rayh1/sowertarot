## The card

An old clockmaker sits at a heavy workbench under an arch of ivy. Between his fingers he holds one small brass gear up to the light, turning it to see every tooth. Beside him stands a tall wooden case with its mechanism open, the other wheels already in place.

The rest of the bench is quiet: an hourglass, a few tools, a small candlestick. Behind him the street of tall grey houses lies in hazy sun. Here, under the arch, the only thing that matters is this gear and where it will go.

## Essence

The Clockmaker is the master who knows that order is built from many small parts, each one made and placed with care. Time cannot be hurried here, only measured and worked with. Mastery comes from thousands of patient movements, most of them too small to notice on their own. The card asks you to choose your own rhythms, and to give your closest attention to what deserves it.

## For today

Look at the gear in his hand. He holds it to the light because he cannot yet know whether it will mesh. The only test is to set it in among the other wheels and turn the case. If the teeth catch, it stays. If they grind, it comes out, and the brass and the hours spent on it are gone. A clockmaker cuts many wheels for every one that runs.

Where are you still studying a single piece, hoping to get it perfect before you try it? A way to open a hard conversation, a fix for something at home, a new order for your mornings. Hold it up once more, then stop studying it.

Make three rough versions today instead of one careful one, and try each against the real thing. Say the opening line three ways out loud. Fit the shelf three ways before you drill. Expect two of them to fail. The effort spent on the wheels that do not catch is the price of finding the one that does.
