## The card

Past the half-open gate, beside the path of pale stones, lies a freshly planted bed. It is new among old things: the rounded hedges around it were shaped over many seasons, and the tall pine beyond it has stood far longer.

On the near side, wild grass and red roses grow as they please. Bees with white wings drift through the gate between the two sides. No one is in the garden. The planting is done, and the gardener has gone.

## Essence

The Garden Gate is a boundary that makes you notice your crossing. It stands between the wild and the tended, and it says that a garden grows in the image of what you care for. Upright, its gift is deliberate attention: you choose where you step and what you tend.

## For today

Look at the bed. The seeds are in. Digging them up to check the roots, adding more, moving them to a better spot: none of that helps now. The soil needs to stay closed for a while. What did you start recently that now needs to be left in the ground?

Name it. It may be a plan you set out, a conversation you began, a habit only a few days old. Today, keep your hands off it. Don't reopen it to see if it works, and don't redo it or explain it again. Walk the path past the bed without kneeling.

Stillness here is a form of care. The hedges grew into their shape while nobody stood over them.
