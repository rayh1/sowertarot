## The card

A barefoot woman in a long indigo dress stands where the waves come in. She holds a silver bowl and tips it, so the water she carried runs back into the sea. Starfish lie on the wet sand at her feet, and rocks line the shore.

Above her a full moon hangs in a clear sky, with stars and a bank of drifting purple clouds. It is night, and the sea moves in its slow rhythm.

## Essence

The Tide is the rhythm of coming and going: giving and receiving, holding and releasing. The moon pulls the water out and brings it back, and both movements belong to the same sea. The water in her bowl was taken from that sea, and pouring it back joins her small part to the larger cycle. Reversed, that rhythm stalls. The bowl stays full. What you took in is kept apart, still and separate, while the sea moves on without it.

## For today

Look at what you are holding in your own bowl. It might be an idea you have not shown anyone, a skill you only use alone, or a part of your life you keep in a separate room from the rest. Held back like this, it feeds nothing, and the tide around you goes on without it.

Ask yourself: where would this water be of use if it joined the rest? Your work might need something you learned from a hobby. A friend's problem might match something you worked out last year.

Then tip the bowl a little. Bring that idea into one conversation, or mix two parts of your day that you usually keep apart. You do not have to empty it all at once. Let some of it run back in, and see what the larger water does with it.
