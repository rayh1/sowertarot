## The card

An old pilgrim walks a path pressed deep into the snow. Seen from above, the path crosses a white meadow, loops once around a dark standing stone, then closes in three tighter rings around it before it runs on toward the far hills.

Every footprint stays. The snow keeps the record of each ring, so the whole winding route lies open on the meadow behind the walker. Bare birches stand at the edges, and the dusk is cold and still.

## Essence

The Spiral is growth that comes back to the same place, each time a little closer to the centre. Nothing here moves in a straight line. The pilgrim circles the stone again and again, and each round is part of the arrival. Reversed, the circling turns heavy. The rings seem to go nowhere, the same lesson comes round once more, and the walker starts to resent the path instead of walking it.

## For today

Look at a round you keep walking: a task you repeat, a habit you rebuild, a question you return to. If it feels like going in circles, notice what the circling leaves behind. A path trodden firm in deep snow is easier for the next walker, even one who arrives long after you have gone on.

So today, ask: who walks more easily because of the track you keep pressing down? Perhaps a colleague who copies your notes, a child who learns your routine, a stranger who finds the work already done.

Walk your round once more with care, even if no one is watching and the stone seems no nearer. Do the repeated part well for its own sake. The rings tighten in their own time; the track you leave is already useful.
