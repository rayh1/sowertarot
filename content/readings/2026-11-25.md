## The card

A bare oak stands against a blue twilight sky, with a pale moon and a few early stars. A young woman in white linen kneels on the grass beside it, one hand held out toward the trunk. Below her, the earth is cut open. The roots spread wide through brown soil, as large as the crown above.

Down the middle runs one thick taproot, lit gold from inside. It is the only warm light on the card. At the bottom it reaches a band of grey stone. It cannot go through. So it fans out across the rock, holding on to it.

## Essence

The Root is the hidden work that holds everything visible up. Reversed, that work has met resistance, and the resistance gets read as a wall. The root seems to stop at the stone. Effort stalls at the hard layer, or it turns back and busies itself higher up, where the soil is soft. The card's answer is in the drawing: where the root cannot go deeper, it spreads, and the stone becomes the thing it grips.

## For today

Think of the place where your own groundwork has gone hard. The skill that stopped improving after the easy months. The basic you skipped and keep working around. The talk with someone close that always runs into the same rock. You may have been spending your effort near the surface instead, because that part still gives.

Go back down to that layer today, on purpose, and stay there long enough to feel the pressure of it. Work the hard part for one focused stretch, with nothing easier open beside it. Expect it to push back. Let it change how you work, not only what you get done: a new grip, a different angle, a slower pace.

What does this stone teach your root to do? If the answer is that you cannot get through, take that as the instruction. Spread out along it. The root does not stop glowing where it meets the rock.
