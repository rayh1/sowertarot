## The card

Look past the gold at the top of the arch, down to the bench. A mason kneels there in a leather apron, his hands resting beside a small golden block. Nobody is watching him. The keystone has just locked into place above, and a thin line of dust drifts down the open archway, but his attention is already on the next piece.

Every stone in this arch passed across a bench like his. Each one was dressed to fit its neighbours, face by face, before anyone could lift it into place.

## Essence

The Keystone is about the one piece that makes a whole hold together. It is set last, and it adds no bulk, only alignment: once it is in, every load turns into strength. But it only works because every stone around it was cut true. Completion here is the reward for fit, and fit is made slowly, by hand.

## For today

Think of something you want to see come together: a project, a plan, a household that keeps slipping out of step. Which single piece of it is still rough? Ask yourself what it would take to shape that one piece properly, rather than to finish the whole.

Then work like the mason. Pick that piece up and give it your full, unhurried attention. Check an edge, take off a little, check it again. Small, careful passes make a stone that fits; one hard blow makes a stone that cracks.

Leave the crown of the arch alone for now. If the pieces below are true, the moment the last one locks will come by itself, and it will hold.
