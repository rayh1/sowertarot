## The card

The room is dark except for one small glow. A single ember rests in white ash in a clay bowl, and a figure with a shadowed face holds an open hand near it, close enough to feel the warmth. A thin thread of smoke climbs straight up, never thicker, never fading.

On the right, a tall arched window holds a pale sky over hills and a pine forest. It is the lightest thing in the room, and the ember's light stops well short of it. The figure keeps its eyes on the bowl. Beyond the glass, the hills run out to where land meets sky.

## Essence

The Ember stands for potential held in waiting: a fire neither out nor alight. Its work is care with judgement. Shelter the glow, feed it when the time comes, let it go when it is spent. The waiting stage has real strength, but only while it leads somewhere.

## For today

Reversed, the waiting has no end date. The coal is kept for later: for a clearer morning, a free month, a version good enough to show. The pale sky in the window looks like a promise of that day. But from inside a room, dawn never finishes arriving. Each time the light grows, the line where land meets sky has moved on, and "ready" has moved with it.

So the condition you set for starting is a far edge. However long you sit, it stays as distant as it is now. That is no fault of yours; it is how a far edge behaves. What helps is a nearer mark.

What have you promised to begin "once things are ready"? Write the condition down in its own words: when I have time, when it is good, when the kids are older. Then cross it out and pick the near hill instead, a point the work can reach by tonight as it stands, rough and small. Reach it. Then go back to the window and note what has come into view from there that was hidden this morning.
