## The card

A travelling player stands at the end of a wooden stage-cart, a carved mask held out in his right hand. Three more masks lie on the boards: one serene, one smiling, one sorrowful. A red cloth hangs over a rail at the back.

Behind all of it is the village green. A few thatched cottages, a line of trees, a soft cream sky. No villager is drawn there, but the village is why the cart stopped at all. Every mask on those boards was carved to be seen from those houses. The show was for them.

## Essence

The Mask is the card of the persona: the many honest faces a person wears to meet the world, and the self who chooses each one. Each role is true without being the whole. Reversed, the role has turned around. Instead of facing the village it faces the player, so the mask is worn for how it looks on him rather than for what it gives the people watching. It is not a lie. It is a role that has forgotten who it serves.

## For today

Look past the player to the cottages. The people in them will never learn his name. What stays with them is whether the comedy made them laugh and the sorrow moved them. Whether the actor looked impressive while doing it matters little to them.

One of your roles may have drifted the same way. The report written to sound thorough instead of to be read. The advice that shows how much you know instead of what the other person needs. The careful politeness that protects you more than it helps anyone. Ask yourself who this role is really for, and when you last played it for them.

Pick one role you will step into today and name the people at the other end of it, by name if you can. Then change one thing in how you play it to fit them: a shorter answer, a plainer word, a question instead of a speech. It may make you look a little less polished. It is the version of the role they came for.
