## The card

At the foot of a great oak lies a small round pond, frozen over. Its pale blue ice holds the tree upside down: every white branch, every frosted twig, the wide crown against the sky. Nothing moves, so the picture in the ice is sharp. Under the ice the water is still there, waiting.

Around the pond the meadow is dry grass and dark seed heads. A robin sits on a low branch at the water's edge, the only warm colour in the scene. Above, the last stars fade and a round moon glows with a soft halo as the sky turns pale toward morning.

## Essence

The Frost is the pause that keeps things safe. Life stops moving for a while, and that stillness protects it rather than ends it. Cold and distance can be a form of care. When movement stops, shapes become clear, the way frost turns a plain branch into a fine white drawing. Growth is not always motion; sometimes it is waiting while time sets things into their true form.

## For today

Ice that ripples shows nothing. The pond can mirror the oak only because it has gone completely still. What in your life would come into focus if you stopped stirring it for a day?

Pick a matter you keep going back to: a plan, a quarrel, a decision. Today, leave your hands off it. Look at it the way the ice looks at the tree. Notice its shape, where it is strong, where it is bare. Name what you see, plainly, without deciding what to do about it yet.

The water under the ice is still alive. Seeing clearly now is how you will know where to move when the thaw comes.
